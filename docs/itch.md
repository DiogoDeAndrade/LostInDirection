# itch.io page

The game's itch.io page (2026-10-26): a Windows build only (no web build, the user's call). The user takes the
screenshots, makes the banner with ChatGPT (the prompt below), then builds and uploads.

## Listing

- Tagline (the short description under the title): "The director is passionate. You don't understand a word he
  says." Others considered: the menu's own, "One actor. One director. Zero understanding."; "Three takes. One furious director. Not a word you understand."; "Read the director's
  body language before he yells CUT!"
- Genre: Adventure.
- Tags (itch takes 10): comedy, funny, parody, 3d, low-poly, zombies, dungeon, short, singleplayer, kenney.
- Inputs: keyboard, gamepad (Xbox and PlayStation).

## Banner prompt (for ChatGPT)

```text
I need a banner image for the top of my game's itch.io page.

THE GAME
"Lost in Direction" is a short comedy adventure game made in a month for a university game jam (the Game Creators
Club at Lusófona University, October 2026), on the theme "Lost in Translation". It's inspired by the scene in Sofia
Coppola's film Lost in Translation (2003) where a tired American actor shoots a whisky commercial in Tokyo: the
director gives him long, passionate directions, and he understands none of it.

In the game you play an actor on a film set. The director only speaks gibberish: his speech balloons are full of
made-up words, like "Bu, bu, bu! Nhi gasu kabagi!". You have to work out what the scene needs from his body
language. He calms down as you get closer to where he wants you, and gets more and more worked up as you wander off
or do the wrong thing. His face is your health: every wrong move sours it, and when it runs out he yells "CUT!" and
you lose a take. The movie has three scenes, each on its own studio set: a zombie movie in a graveyard at night
(you dig up a grave, a zombie climbs out and chases you to the crypt), a dark dungeon maze with spike traps, and a
temple where you fight waves of orcs with a bow to free a girl from a cage.

VISUAL STYLE
Match the game, which is built from Kenney's low-poly 3D asset packs: chunky, toy-like, simple low-poly models with
flat colours and almost no texture, small chibi-proportioned characters with big heads, soft stylised lighting. Not
realistic, not pixel art, not anime. The sets are dark and moody (night, candles, warm orange firelight, cold blue
moonlight), and it's clearly a film set: studio spotlights on stands, the edge of the set's floor, a film camera.

THE CHARACTERS
- The director: a blocky, square-headed character (like a Minecraft figure, bigger and built differently from the
  others), with a black beret, a thick black moustache and a red scarf. He waves his arms passionately, mid-rant,
  with a speech balloon full of gibberish.
- The actor (the player): a small chibi character in a dark suit and tie, standing on the graveyard set and holding
  a shovel. He looks deadpan and completely lost (a small "?" over his head is fine). He shouldn't look like any
  real person.
- A zombie: a small green chibi zombie, shambling towards him from an open grave.
- Optional, small, in the background: a film clapperboard.

COMPOSITION
A wide landscape banner. It will be cropped to 960x320 (3:1), so keep everything important (the title and the
characters) inside the middle horizontal band, and leave the top and bottom as background that can be cut off. Put
the director on the left, his speech balloon towards the middle, and the actor with the zombie on the graveyard set
on the right (or another arrangement that reads as clearly). Fade the image's edges into a dark background colour
(near black, slightly purple, about #15131C) so the banner blends into the page.

TEXT
The title "LOST IN DIRECTION", big and readable, in a heavy condensed sans-serif (like Barlow Condensed Black),
white with a dark outline. The balloon reads "Bu, bu, bu! Nhi gasu kabagi!". No other text, logos or watermarks.
```

## Page text

```text
LOST IN DIRECTION

You're an actor on a film set, and the director is very passionate about this scene. The problem: you don't
understand a single word he says.

Walk around the set, try out the props and keep an eye on the director in the corner of the screen. Work out what
he wants before he runs out of patience.

Made in a month for the October 2026 Game Creators Club at Lusófona University, on the theme "Lost in
Translation". The idea comes from the scene in Sofia Coppola's film where Bill Murray shoots a whisky commercial
and doesn't understand a word his director says.

HOW TO PLAY

- The director, in the bottom-left corner, only speaks gibberish. Read his body language instead: he calms down as
  you get closer to where he wants you and face the right thing, and he gets worked up when you wander off, dawdle
  or look at the wrong thing.
- His face is your health. The right action cheers him up; the wrong one, or keeping him waiting, sours him. When
  his mood runs out: "CUT!", and the scene starts again.
- You have 3 takes for the whole movie (the slates, top right). Lose the last one and you're fired.
- To interact, walk up to something and face it: its options appear. Cycle through them and pick one.
- The movie has three scenes: a zombie flick, a dungeon maze and a temple rescue. The director doesn't like having
  zombies close to his star, and watch your step in the maze.

CONTROLS (a gamepad is recommended)

Move: left stick / WASD or arrow keys
Turn on the spot, aim the bow: right stick (on the keyboard, turn by moving)
Do the selected option, skip the director's lines: X (Xbox) or Square (PlayStation) / E
Next option: Y or Triangle / Q
Shoot, once you have the bow: either trigger / Space or left mouse button
Menus: d-pad or left stick / WASD or arrow keys; A or X / Enter to confirm; B / Esc to go back

Windows only.
```
